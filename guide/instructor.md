# Facilitation and learning assessment

## Before the session

Open lab 00 on the browser and network participants will use. Allow time for the first Python/package download. Download the Book pages or lab 08's briefing as an alternative. Pair participants with complementary public-health, GIS and programming experience. Ask them to agree on a reporting window before comparing results.

The core exercise is fictional; participants should not upload patient records into a public demonstration. Adapt the examples using approved aggregate teaching data. Maintain the distinction between upstream live context and local evidence.

## Learning objectives and evidence

| Objective | Labs | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Trace a signal to source and time | 00, 01, 09 | Source register with mode and timestamps |
| Select an appropriate geographic display | 03, 04 | 2D map with units and a justified 3D use |
| Explain numerators, denominators and missingness | 05, 06 | Report-date curve, table and uncertainty statement |

| Communicate a reviewable assessment | 08, 11 | Dated briefing bundle and peer review |
| Share methods accessibly | 10 | Static/text alternative and workshop plan |

## Suggested timing

For an introductory 90-minute session, use 00, selected cells from 03 and 06, then 08. For a half-day, add 05 and 07. For a full course, use all twelve labs and reserve 60–90 minutes for the capstone. Each notebook includes its own expected duration, practice prompts and interpretation notes.

## Misconceptions to surface

| Learner statement | Facilitation prompt |
|---|---|
| “The darkest district needs the most resources.” | Which denominator, coverage and access constraints support that claim? |
| “There were zero cases when the report disappeared.” | Where is the evidence of a submitted zero-case report? |
| “Flood and illness are correlated, so this proves the cause.” | What was built into the generator? What confounding or measurement bias would matter in real data? |
| “The globe is more accurate because it is 3D.” | Which question does it answer? What is hidden behind the sphere? |
| “The score tells the incident manager what to do.” | Who chose the weights, and how would local knowledge change the decision? |
| “My notebook link shares my edits.” | Can a colleague see those edits, or only the published starting notebook? |

## Debrief and feedback

Use the capstone's 12-point rubric. Require another participant to recompute one number and identify one unresolved gap. Accept different priority orders when the reasoning, assumptions and verification plan are explicit. Have teams revise their briefing after the challenge, retaining a version and change note.

The source-inspired coordination context is the [WHO EOC framework](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/framework-for-a-public-health-emergency-operations-centre). The exercises, rubric and schedules here are original educational materials, not certification requirements or an agency-approved operating procedure.
